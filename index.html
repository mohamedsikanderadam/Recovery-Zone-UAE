The studio

Not a spa menu. A treatment plan.

Recovery Zone UAE is a clinician-led recovery and mobility studio. Three zones sit under one roof: massage for hands-on soft tissue work, the contrast zone for sauna and cold, and consultation and stretch for assessment and mobility.

You do not pick a treatment off a board and hope. A therapist assesses first — what you train, what hurts, what has stopped moving — then the session is built from what they find. Same philosophy whether you are mid-season, mid-rehab, or just sitting at a desk too long.

Zone 01

Massage

Five hands-on treatments. Which one you get is decided at assessment, and a session can move between them if the tissue asks for it.

  • Deep Tissue Release

    Slow, sustained pressure into the deeper layers. For chronic tightness that surface work never reaches.

  • Sports Massage

    Built around training load — before competition, after it, and for the overuse strain in between.

  • Full Body Massage

    Head-to-toe coverage when the load is general rather than local, and nothing single is flaring.

  • Relaxation Massage

    Lighter, continuous work aimed at down-regulating a nervous system stuck in high gear.

  • Trigger Point Massage

    Direct pressure held on specific referring points until they release. Precise, local, and deliberately uncomfortable.

Zone 02

The Contrast Zone

Heat, cold, and the swing between the two. Used after training, between sessions, or as a protocol in its own right.

Sauna

Up to 5 people

Dry heat to raise core temperature and open circulation. Booked alone or as the first half of a contrast protocol.

Ice Bath

Up to 2 people

Controlled cold immersion with a therapist setting the exposure. For post-session load and inflammation management.

Contrast Bath

Sauna + ice bath protocol

Alternating heat and cold in timed rounds. The cycle is prescribed — how many rounds and how long is not left to you.

Massage Chair

Between rounds or standalone

Mechanical percussion and compression. Useful as a warm-down between contrast rounds or on a light recovery day.

Zone 03

Consultation & Stretch

Where the studio works out what you actually need. Nothing else gets booked blind.

  • Consultation area — history, training load, and a movement screen. Ten minutes that decide the rest of the session.
  • Deep Stretch area — therapist-guided stretching that takes joints through range you cannot reach on your own.
  • Mobility work — loaded drills to hold the range you just gained, so it survives the week.

Assessment first, always.

In detail

Seven hands-on treatments

Delivered by qualified therapists and chosen for what your body is actually doing — training, rehabilitating, or sitting still for too long. Working pressure is listed so you know what you are walking into.

Deep Tissue MassageDeep

Slow, sustained pressure worked into the deeper muscle layers. For chronic tightness that surface work never reaches — long-standing back, neck and calf load that has stopped responding to anything lighter.

Sports MassageFirm

Built around training load: preparation before competition, recovery after it, and management of overuse strain in between. Paced to where you are in your week, not to a fixed routine.

Lymphatic Drainage MassageLight

Light, rhythmic strokes worked in the direction of lymph flow. Used for post-training and post-operative swelling and fluid retention, where pressure would only make things worse.

Assisted StretchingLight

Therapist-guided stretching that takes joints through range you cannot reach on your own. Stiff hips, locked shoulders, short hamstrings — held long enough to change something.

Myofascial ReleaseModerate

Sustained tension held on restricted fascia until it gives. The aim is glide between tissue layers rather than forcing the muscle underneath.

Myofascial DecompressionNegative

Cupping-based negative pressure. Tissue is lifted away from the layers beneath instead of being compressed into them — useful over stubborn adhesions and scar tissue.

Kinetic Release TherapyFirm

Pressure applied while the limb is moved, so tissue releases through the pattern it actually has to work in — not while lying still.
